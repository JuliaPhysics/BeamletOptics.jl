Automatic differentiation ​

Results of solve_system! can be differentiated with respect to the parameters of a setup via forward-mode automatic differentiation with ForwardDiff.jl, e.g. for gradient-based optimization or sensitivity analysis. The derivative is exact up to the accuracy of the ray tracing, typically better than relative, and it is free of the step size problem of finite differences.

Usage ​

BMO is generic in its number type. Write the setup as a function of the parameter and build every component, beam and detector in the number type T of the parameter, which is a ForwardDiff.Dual while the derivative is computed:

julia
using BeamletOptics, ForwardDiff

# x coordinate of the spot behind a lens that is shifted laterally by `dx`
function spot_x(dx)
    T = typeof(dx)
    lens = SphericalLens(T(50e-3), T(-50e-3), T(5e-3), T(25e-3), _ -> T(1.5))
    translate3d!(lens, [dx, zero(T), zero(T)])
    pd = Detector(T(40e-3))
    translate3d!(pd, T[0, 0.1, 0])
    beam = Beam(T[0, -0.05, 0], T[0, 1, 0], T(1e-6))
    solve_system!(System([lens, pd]), beam)
    return BeamletOptics.hit_point(only(BeamletOptics.hits(pd)))[1]
end

ForwardDiff.derivative(spot_x, 0.0)   # ≈ 1.90

The parameter can enter anywhere: the pose of a component (translate3d!, rotate3d!), its geometry (radii, thicknesses), a refractive index (e.g. _ -> n0 for the parameter n0) or a source. Many parameters at once are differentiated with ForwardDiff.gradient or ForwardDiff.jacobian.

The test suite (test/TestAutoDiff.jl) compares the derivatives with finite differences for rays and beamlets through spherical, aspheric, meniscus and cylindrical lenses, spherical, parabolic and conic mirrors, prisms (including total internal reflection), thin, plate and cube beamsplitters, polarizing filters, for polarized rays and for the optical power on a detector, e.g. of a Michelson interferometer vs. the position of a mirror.

Limitations ​

  • Discrete events are not differentiable. Which surface a ray hits, the onset of total internal reflection, a ray that misses an aperture or a detector, and the cutoff by r_max change the result in steps. The derivative is that of the current path; at a step it is not defined, also not for finite differences.

  • Edges and corners. At the edge of a prism or the rim of a lens the normal changes abruptly, a ray that hits such an edge has no meaningful derivative.

  • Number type of object groups. An ObjectGroup is Float64 unless its number type is passed: ObjectGroup(objects, T).

  • Forward mode only. The cost grows with the number of parameters. Reverse-mode AD (Zygote, Enzyme) is not supported, since the tracing mutates its beams and objects.

  • Compile time. Each new combination of dual number and setup is compiled on its first call, which takes several seconds.
